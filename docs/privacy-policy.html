Privacy Policy

IPTV Brother Player

A player you supply your own playlist to. This page explains, precisely, what the app sends off your device and what never leaves it.

Effective 15 August 2026 Applies to both the sideload and store builds of IPTV Brother Player Developer Alex Brooks, MB

What we collect

  • Anonymous product analytics — screens viewed, and a short list of app events like "a playlist synced" or "playback failed"
  • Standard device/session technical data attached to those events (device model, OS version, locale, timezone)
  • Sideload build only, and only if you say yes: your IP address and approximate location, to route bandwidth through Pawns.app

What we never collect

  • Your name, email, or any account — there is no account
  • Your playlist URL, provider login, or password
  • Which channels or shows you actually watch
  • Screen recordings of you using the app
  • Anything to sell to advertisers — there are no ads

The short version

IPTV Brother Player plays a playlist you supply — an M3U URL, a local file, or an Xtream Codes login. It ships with no channels and no content of its own. Because of that, most of what a typical app's privacy policy has to cover simply doesn't apply here: there's no account to create, no profile to build, and the app has no server of its own that your playlist or viewing activity passes through.

What this policy actually covers is two optional, separately-controlled things: anonymous product analytics (so the developer can tell whether the app works), and an opt-in bandwidth-sharing feature that funds development, present only in the version distributed outside app stores.

Usage & device analytics

Present in every build, sending data only if the developer's build was configured with an analytics key.

The app uses PostHog, a product-analytics service, to understand which parts of the app get used and whether things like adding a playlist or playing a channel actually work. Each install is identified by a random ID generated on your device — not your name, email, or any account, because none exists.

Standard technical data

Every analytics event carries the usual technical context a device sends with any network request:

  • Device manufacturer and model, screen size, Android version
  • App version and build number
  • Locale and time zone
  • Coarse network type (Wi-Fi / cellular / Bluetooth on or off — not your IP-based location)

What's tracked, and why

The list is deliberately short. A settings toggle or a channel change doesn't get logged — only the handful of moments below:

EventWhat it records
screenWhich section of the app you navigated to (Live TV, Guide, Settings, etc.) — not what's on screen within it
source_addedThat a playlist was added, and by which method (URL / file / Xtream) — never the URL or login itself
source_syncedWhether a playlist sync succeeded, and a channel/movie/series count — never channel names or a provider hostname
content_playedThe kind of thing played (live / movie / series / catch-up) — never which one
playback_failedA stable error category (e.g. "timeout") for something that visibly failed to play — never the stream URL
favorite_toggledThat a favourite was added or removed — not which channel
parental_pin_setWhether a parental PIN is enabled — never the PIN
sharing_consent / sharing_toggleWhether bandwidth sharing was accepted, declined, or later switched — see below

Screen recording of app usage is permanently disabled in the code, not merely unconfigured — the screen you spend the most time on is a full-screen video player, and recording it would be a proxy for your actual viewing habits well beyond what the events above capture.

Internet bandwidth sharing

Sideload build only. Never present in the version submitted to app stores.

The version of IPTV Brother Player distributed as a direct APK download can use a portion of your device's idle internet bandwidth to route traffic for Pawns.app and its clients, as a way to fund development. This is off until you explicitly say yes, on a dialog shown the first time you open the app — never enabled silently, and never on by default.

If you accept, Pawns.app receives your IP address and approximate location, and it runs as a visible background service with a permanent notification for as long as it's active. You can turn it off at any time from Settings → Internet sharing, which stops the service immediately. Declining, or switching it off later, is remembered — you are never asked again automatically.

Pawns.app is a third party with its own data practices, separate from this app's: read Pawns.app's privacy policy for what it does with that data.

The build submitted to the Play Store and the Amazon Appstore does not include this SDK at all — not merely disabled, but not present in that build's app package — because both stores prohibit apps that route other users' traffic through a device.

What stays on your device

Never transmitted anywhere by this app.

  • Your playlist and provider details — the M3U URL, file, or Xtream server/username/password you enter, plus the channel and programme-guide data imported from it, live only in the app's local database.
  • Provider credentials are encrypted at rest, with a key generated in the Android Keystore that is non-exportable and, on supported hardware, never enters app memory in the clear.
  • The diagnostics log (Settings → Diagnostics log) — a rolling record of the last 300 network and playback events, kept only in memory to help you or a provider debug a stream that won't play. It is never uploaded, and any credentials are redacted before they're shown even to you.
  • An exported backup (Settings → Backup) never includes provider passwords — an imported login has to be re-entered after a restore, by design.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of the above. There is nothing left behind on any server, because none of it was ever sent to one.

Children's privacy

IPTV Brother Player is a general-audience player, not directed at children, and the app does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone. Because the app plays whatever a user supplies, it includes parental controls (Settings → Parental controls) that let a parent lock categories a playlist marks as adult behind a PIN.

Your choices

  • Decline or withdraw bandwidth-sharing consent any time in Settings — see above.
  • Clear the diagnostics log or your watch history from Settings → Data.
  • Uninstall the app to remove everything it stored, locally and permanently.
  • For anything analytics has recorded against your device's random install ID, contact the developer using the details below — as an anonymous ID with no name or email behind it, a specific request may only be actionable as "stop sending data from this device," which uninstalling already accomplishes.

Changes to this policy

If what the app collects changes, this page will be updated and the "Effective" date above will change with it. Material changes — a new category of data, or a new third party — will also be reflected in the app's release notes.
